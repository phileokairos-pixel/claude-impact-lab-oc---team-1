# Recuperative Care Admission Criteria and Eligibility Outline (adults)

Transcribed from the document Illumination Health + Home (IHH) gave us for this event. The wording
is IHH's, typos included. Section order follows the original. If this page and IHH staff in the
room disagree, IHH staff are right.

The same rules with stable IDs are in [`admission-criteria.json`](admission-criteria.json).

## Which document wins

IHH has two documents that describe eligibility, written about two years apart for different
readers:

| Document | Written | For | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| This outline | November 2023 | IHH's own clinical staff | Detailed, with judgment calls ("case by case") |
| [Referral form](referral-form-and-checklist.md) (form RC0725) | July 2025, revised October 2025 | Hospital case managers | Short, simplified, stricter |

**When the two disagree, this outline wins.** It is the internal rulebook coordinators apply. The
form is the summary hospitals see. Where this outline is silent and the form lists an exclusion
(tuberculosis, refusing detox), the form's exclusion is the only written rule, so apply it.

In practice coordinators also fill gaps from experience. The table at the end of this page lists
where the written rules run out. A good tool shows the coordinator the gap instead of guessing.

## General admission criteria

Meets one or more of the following:

- Homeless/at risk of homelessness and too ill or frail to recover from illness or injury
- Lives alone with no formal supports and too ill or frail to recover from illness or injury
- At risk of hospitalization or re-hospitalization after hospitalization

### ADLs

- Able to complete all Activities of Daily Living (ADLs) independently including personal hygiene
- Patient must be able to complete personal hygiene independently. Benches are available for
  bathing so seated shower is acceptable. Patient must be able to gather all of their own
  bathing/showering supplies, transfer to shower area and perform shower/bath independently
- Able to toilet independently and/or change briefs/diapers independently

### Mobility

- If using DME (wheelchair, walker, etc) must be able to use independently
- PT/OT notes must be sent for review
- Patient must be able to ambulate at least 100 ft (rest breaks are acceptable) at modified
  independence if not wheelchair dependent
- Wheelchair dependent patients must be able to transfer independently

### Mental health

- Patient must be mentally stable on medications
- No suicidal ideation
- No commanding auditory or visual hallucinations
- If a patient was admitted with acute alcohol intoxication, the patient must be held for at least
  72 hours and not have signs of alcohol withdrawal (maintain a CIWA-AR score of <8, if being
  assessed with this tool) prior to admission to recuperative care.
- Cannot qualify, have history of or currently be under a conservatorship or FSP

### Cognition

- Must be capable of self-administering medications (must confirm that patient can self-administer
  insulin) and receiving medical education
- AOx4

### Other factors

- Ineligible if client is verbally or physically aggressive towards staff or other patients
- Patients that are registered sex offenders will be evaluated on a case-by-case basis (however, as
  a general guide, any offense that involves violence will likely be denied
- Patients with IV access can be accepted if they have home health established to maintain the IV
  and administer the medication as needed. There is no limit on time for IV
- Cannot accept patients on isolation unless isolation status is cleared prior to admission to
  recuperative care

### End of life care

- Hospice patients are eligible for recuperative care admission. Hospice services must be arranged
  prior to admission to recuperative care and patient, family and other involved parties must be
  made aware that recuperative care staff cannot administer medications, these medications will be
  administered by hospice staff

### Infection control

- MRSA swab, documented negative prior to recuperative care admission if coming from inpatient
  hospitalization
- Covid swab (rapid swab acceptable) documented negative within 24 hours of recuperative care
  admission if coming from inpatient admission

## Specific disease processes

### Diabetes

- If patient is being referred from inpatient hospitalization, 5 consecutive glucose readings
  should be sent. Readings must be show improvement and stabilization.
- If admitted from the emergency department, it must be established and documented that the
  patient is not in DKA.
- We will not accept a diabetic patient that was started on an insulin regimen less than 24 hours
  prior to admission.
- Poorly controlled diabetics on an oral medication or established insulin regimen may be accepted
  from the ED if they have follow up with a primary care provider scheduled within the next 5-7
  days, have not had an episode of hypoglycemia within the last week and have a written treatment
  plan for glucose control (i.e. sliding scale parameters, medication hold guidelines, etc).
- Patient should understand how to use glucometer
- Patient should understand insulin administration, if applicable

Associated questions:

- Does the patient use insulin? Is the insulin administered through a pump?
- Does the patient take oral medication?
- What was the patient's last hemoglobin A1C? This is not a limiting factor for admission but does
  provide important information about the patient's baseline glucose level over *(sentence is cut
  off in the source)*

### Infection

- The last 5 sets of vital signs should be sent. Patient is ineligible for recuperative care if VS
  show signs of sepsis (continued fever, elevated heart rate, low blood pressure)
- The patient should be able to administer oral antibiotics independently
- If IV antibiotics are required, home health care should be ordered before admission to
  recuperative care to maintain IV and administer antibiotics

Associated questions:

- Location of infection?
- Were cultures performed? Results should be sent
- Is the patient on isolation?

### Respiratory illness

- Chest imaging should be sent (Chest XR, CT, MRI) when available
- The last 5 sets of vital signs should be sent, if available; O2 saturation may be chronically low
  if patient has a history of COPD, readings should be stable and consistent with patient's normal
  O2 saturation
- If O2 is required, the hospital must order it and have the tank ready to be sent to the site the
  day before the client arrives at the recuperative care site
- Respiratory therapy notes should be sent

Associated questions:

- Is the patient receiving any breathing treatments? If so, what are they?
- Is the patient on antibiotics for this condition?

### Cardiac

- The last 5 sets of vital signs should be sent. Patient's BP should be stable on oral medication
  for at least 24 hours
- If patient had a troponin in the hospital, at least 3 consecutive results should be sent with at
  least 2 negative results
- EKG results should be sent
- If patient has a history of atrial fibrillation or has been diagnosed with atrial fibrillation,
  patient should be able to take anticoagulation therapy independently and understand any
  associated lab work (i.e. INR) necessary to track this medication
- Ejection Fraction of 25% or higher may be accepted as long as this has been established to be the
  patient's baseline. If the patient is on chemotherapy or is being immediately discharged from
  hospitalization for heart attack with a lowered ejection fraction, this patient is ineligible for
  recuperative care

Associated questions:

- Has the patient required IV anti-hypertensive medications within the last 24 hours? Patient will
  not be accepted for admission to recuperative care if IV blood pressure medications have been
  required within 24 hours

## Eligible and ineligible conditions by body system

### Integumentary (skin and wounds)

Eligible:

- Uncomplicated infections of the skin, cellulitis or abscess, requiring oral antibiotic treatments
- Wound care to be coordinated with home health agency prior to arrival at recuperative care and
  should coincide with the frequency of dressing changes or wound monitoring. If a patient should
  have a dressing change or wound check within 24 hours of discharge from the hospital, wound care
  should be arranged to arrive to recuperative care within 24 hours. Additionally, all wounds in
  hard-to-reach areas (coccyx, , back of thighs, back, perineum) will require at least one visit
  from wound care in the recuperative care setting regardless of patient's ability to change the
  dressing in a hospital setting.
- IV antibiotics are allowed as long as home health has been coordinated to maintain the IV and
  either patient is able to administer their own IV antibiotics or home health will be
  administering the antibiotic
- Uncomplicated wounds (e.g. laceration without infection)
- Post-surgical incision. If staples or external sutures are placed, appropriate follow up should
  be scheduled for removal prior to admission to recuperative care
- Stage 1, 2, 3 pressure ulcers. Wound care to be coordinated and scheduled prior to admission to
  recuperative care
- Third degree burns that affect <15% of the body. Wound care to be coordinated with home health
  agency prior to admission to recuperative care

Ineligible:

- Complicated wounds requiring skin grafting
- Complicated large abscess
- Burns associated with inhalation injury

### Ophthalmology

Eligible:

- Uncomplicated infections of the eye
- Post-op glaucoma surgery
- Cataract surgery requiring extraction with lens implant (uncomplicated situation)
- Diabetic retinopathy
- Diabetic macular edema
- Post operative admission for vitreous hemorrhage, if patient meets all other criteria
- Post operative admission for retinal detachment, if patient meets all other criteria

### Respiratory

Eligible:

- Pneumonia
- Bronchitis
- COPD not requiring continuous oxygen therapy
- Asthma
- Patients with O2 baseline of 94% or greater with physician explanation
- Patients with mature tracheostomy who are able to independently care for their tracheostomy site
  and are also able to eat and use a speaking valve
- CPAP

Ineligible:

- Pneumothorax
- Hemothorax
- Trach patients with ventilator
- Pulmonary Embolism
- Respiratory conditions requiring BiPAP

### Musculoskeletal

Eligible:

- Closed fractures
- PT to be coordinated and scheduled prior to arrival at recuperative care
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Non-elective hip/knee replacements following SNF placement, if patient meets the other
  recuperative care guidelines

Ineligible:

- Open fractures
- Osteomyelitis
- External fixation devices
- Immediately following hospitalization for amputation with wounds
- Facial trauma with mandibular or maxillary fracture requiring jaw wiring

### GI

Eligible:

- Post abdominal surgical care
- Uncomplicated inguinal or umbilical hernia
- Diverticulitis
- Uncomplicated inflammatory bowel disease
- Ascites, if chronic
- Gastroesophageal reflux disorder
- Post hospitalization for uncomplicated bowel obstruction that did not require surgical
  intervention

Ineligible:

- Decompensated cirrhosis (defined by the development of clinically evident complications of portal
  hypertension, ascites, variceal hemorrhage, hepatic encephalopathy or liver insufficiency,
  jaundice)
- Immediately following hospitalization for bleeding ulcer disease or esophageal varices
- C.Diff

### Endocrine

Eligible:

- Diabetes that shows improvement and stabilization in glucose levels as seen on at least 5
  consecutive glucose readings with medical treatment (see the diabetes rules above)
- Hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism on medical treatment
- s/p thyroidectomy

Ineligible:

- Immediately s/p parathyroidectomy
- Alcoholic ketoacidosis
- DKA

### GU

Eligible:

- Urinary tract infection
- Post-acute glomerulonephritis, if caused by an infection
- Post-op laparoscopic gynecologic surgery for treatment of non-cancerous conditions
- Urolithiasis
- Patients requiring catheterization if confirmation is provided that patient is able to
  self-catheterize
- Indwelling catheter if patient is able to empty on their own and follow up has been scheduled for
  removal
- Patients requiring dialysis if dialysis schedule and frequency has been established

Ineligible:

- Patients requiring intermittent catheterization that has to be performed by medical staff

### Circulatory system

Eligible:

- Post-op angiogram
- Cardiac catheterization/stent placement
- Pacemaker/ICD insertion
- Stable hypertension (patient with baseline elevated BP; SBP<170, DBP<100, asymptomatic and not
  currently taking medication but with established follow up with a primary care provider)
- Post coronary artery bypass graft or heart valve surgery with controlled, stable blood pressure,
  normal hemoglobin/hematocrit, normal EF and without arrhythmias. Home health must be ordered and
  scheduled prior to admission to recuperative care. Cardiac surgery follow up must be scheduled
  prior to admission to recuperative care.
- Hypertension that responds well to oral anti-hypertensive regimen
- History of heart attack. If being admitted to recuperative care immediately following admission
  for heart attack, will only accept patients who have an normal ejection fraction

Ineligible:

- Unstable cardiac arrhythmias
- Hypertension that requires IV medications to improve, is symptomatic or is not responding to oral
  medication

### Neurology

Eligible:

- Alcohol withdrawal seizure, following detox/stabilization
- Post carpal tunnel or cubital release
- Mild traumatic brain injury with intact ADLs
- Cervical or lumbar radiculopathy following epidural injection or decompression surgery

Ineligible:

- Stroke syndrome with neurological residual/deficit (e.g. ambulatory difficulty, seizure)
- New onset of seizure with neurological deficit/impairment
- Moderate or severe traumatic brain injury
- Cervical or lumbar fusion
- Dementia
- Immediately s/p neurosurgery. Neurosurgical patients who meet other eligibility criteria should
  be escalated to further clinical review

## Places where the written rules are unclear

Real rulebooks have gaps. A good tool shows the coordinator the gap instead of guessing.

| Where | What is unclear |
|---|---|
| Registered sex offenders | The referral form says not eligible; this document says case by case. **Settled: case by case, with offenses involving violence likely denied.** Flag for the coordinator; never auto-deny. |
| Wheelchair users | The referral form asks for "walking 100 ft. with or without DME." **Settled: this document applies.** A wheelchair-dependent patient who transfers independently and uses the chair independently is eligible. |
| Tuberculosis; refusing detox | Listed as exclusions on the referral form, not mentioned here. The form's exclusion applies. |
| "O2 baseline of 94% or greater with physician explanation" | Transcribed as written. Meaning in practice not stated. |
| Last A1C | The sentence is cut off in the source. |
| "FSP" | Not spelled out in the source. Likely Full Service Partnership, a county mental-health program. |
| Insulin "less than 24 hours prior to admission" | Read as admission to recuperative care. The document does not say whether the clock starts at the first dose or when doses stop changing. |
| DKA | Listed as ineligible. The document does not say whether DKA that has resolved still excludes. |
| C. diff | Listed as ineligible. The document does not say whether that ends when isolation is lifted or when treatment finishes. |
| Troponin after a heart attack | The cardiac rules ask for at least two negative troponins. A patient admitted for a heart attack will not have them, yet history of heart attack is on the eligible list. |
| COVID swab "within 24 hours of admission" | A swab that is negative when the referral is sent can be more than 24 hours old by arrival. |
| "Complicated large abscess" | Not defined. |
| Hard-to-reach wounds | The list reads "coccyx, , back of thighs, back, perineum". An item seems to be missing, and the sacrum is not named. |
| Home health | The threshold changes from rule to rule: "established", "ordered", "coordinated", "coordinated and scheduled". |
| Not yet | The outcomes are approve or deny. There is no stated "re-refer when ready" for a patient who will qualify in a day or two. |
